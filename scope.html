Gown Rental Ledger

You named seven fields: name, date, date of party, number, price, paid, gown description and number. That's a booking ledger, not the ten-phase rental platform I scoped first. This version is built around those seven fields, plus just enough system that the ledger can't lie to you.

01

What this is

Every rental is one row. Every gown is a numbered physical thing. The app won't let you promise the same gown to two parties on the same weekend. It runs in a browser on your own hardware — one shop, one login, no branding.

Six tables, not thirty-eight

Rental · Gown · GownPhoto · Payment · User · AppSetting. Everything in v1 that those seven fields don't need is parked in §08 — on record, not built.

Still a clean subset

Nothing here forecloses v1. If you later want sizes, fittings, cleaning or a storefront, they layer on top — the data model is a narrower version of the same shape, not a different one.

"Number" — I built both

Every rental gets an auto Rental #, and there's a separate Phone field. Whichever you meant, it's there, and neither one costs anything.

"Paid" — an amount, not a checkbox

Stored as money with the balance computed. A checkbox is just the case where Paid equals Price — so this covers both readings and also answers "who still owes me money" without a second system.

If Date should mean the pickup date rather than the write-up date, that's a one-line change. Say so and I'll flip it.

03

Gowns

A gown is a numbered physical object, not just text in a row. This is the only thing I've added beyond your list, and it's what makes Gown # mean something.

The gown record

  • Gown # — the tag number on the garment
  • Description, size, color
  • Photos — one or more, uploaded from a phone
  • Default price — auto-fills on new rentals
  • Condition — New · Good · Fair · Retired
  • Status — derived: In Out Due back
  • History — every rental it's been on, and what it's earned

The double-booking check

Pick a gown for a party date and the app checks whether it's already promised anywhere near that date. If it is, you get a warning naming the other rental and its party date.

You can override it — sometimes you know something the app doesn't — but you'll never do it by accident.

This is the most valuable code in the build, and it only exists because gowns are numbered records instead of typed-in text.

06

Stack

Same as the CAF CRM — proven on this hardware, and I can lift working files straight across.

Built on

  • Next.js 15 · React 19 · TypeScript
  • PostgreSQL via Prisma — its own database, nothing shared with CAF
  • One globals.css with custom properties. No Tailwind, no component library
  • Its own Docker container on the Pi, its own port, LAN-only

Lifted from the CRM

  • lib/auth.ts — signed session cookie login
  • lib/file-attachments.ts — gown photos
  • lib/csv.ts · spreadsheet.ts — CSV and Excel export
  • lib/reference-numbers.ts — the rental numbering
  • app-shell.tsx · ui.tsx — nav and badges, restyled to §05
